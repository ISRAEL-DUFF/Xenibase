Billing turns recorded usage into invoices, posts every movement of money to a double-entry ledger, and collects from each organisation's wallet at the wallet provider. Prices are in US dollars and money moves in naira: an invoice is converted when it is issued, at a rate frozen on it. Three neighbours share the page because they spend or save money for the platform: the Free tier pauses and archives idle projects, capacity automation buys and deletes servers, and cost attribution says what each organisation costs. Legal documents record what organisations agreed to.

Every loop here runs on every xenibase-server without a leader lock: billing.Run every 10 minutes, capacity.Run every 30 seconds. They rely on idempotency keys, unique indexes and row locks rather than on running once.

Money and currencies

No amount passes through a float between a usage record and an invoice (decision M20 #1). Money is int64 in minor units; unit prices, quantities and rates are Dec, a math/big.Rat carried in JSON as a string. Each invoice line is rounded once, half away from zero:

AmountUnit
Price book fees and unit pricesCents of USD (fractions allowed), or kobo for an NGN book
Invoices, ledger, payments, credit notes, budget, spend capKobo; invoices keep the dollar figures beside them (usd_*)
Infrastructure costs; the capacity budgetThe cost's own currency as fractional float64; minor units of the budget's currency

A platform with a wallet provider starts on DefaultPrices in dollars; without one there is no rate, so it starts on DefaultPricesNGN. Both currencies have two decimals, so kobo per cent is naira per dollar (FXQuote.Naira).

Spec and code differ

V3 §3.3 prices in kobo. Prices moved to dollars with the wallet provider (migration 00051, docs/billing.md); what moves stays in kobo.

Price books, plans and accounts

A price book is one JSON document per version (Prices): the currency, each plan's monthly and annual fee, quota plan, allowances and unit prices, the dedicated hourly rates, and the add-ons. Validate requires free, pro and team, a Free plan without fee or unit prices, no negative prices and premiums of at most 500%. Publish needs an effective date at least 30 days ahead (except the first book) and emails each affected organisation what changes; the price_book_frozen trigger refuses changes to a published row. Reprice moves organisations to the book in effect, except grandfathered ones and annual terms, which move at renewal.

Dedicated instances, HA standbys (plus the HA premium) and read replicas are priced per vCPU-, RAM- and disk-hour whatever the plan; PITR and retention add-ons by the hour while set; a region premium as a percentage of a project's hardware lines there (regionPremium, V4.1 §4.3).

Account creates an organisation's billing_accounts row on first need, on the current book. It holds the plan and term, the mode, the book and grandfathering, business details, WHT applicability, payment terms, budget, spend cap and forecast, the dunning state, the wallet id and the debit consent. Owners and billing members see it, admins do not (). Prepaid, the V3 mode of daily deductions from a credit balance (DeductPrepaid), is refused for an organisation with a wallet (switchMode).

The ledger

Every money movement is a Txn: a key, a source, and two or more entries whose debits equal their credits.

A key already posted with the same number of entries is a no-op; anything else is an error. The database holds the invariants whatever the code does:

Usage and rating

RecordUsage takes an advisory lock, records each completed hour since its watermark (redoing the last), each completed day of backup storage, and upserts usage_records, so a rerun changes nothing; rows older than 90 days roll up to days. Invoicing waits for the same watermark (usageRecordedThrough). Plan metrics (PlanMetrics: storage, backups, branch hours, transfer, webhook deliveries, job runs and the backend services) are rated above the plan's allowance; dedicated, HA, replica and add-on hours per project; SMS and WhatsApp codes at the provider's kobo cost plus a margin, in kobo even on a dollar book (nativeNaira). Branch storage, webhook retries and replica hours are not charged on their own.

rate splits the month at applied plan changes, puts each day's usage in its segment, and rates each segment against its plan's allowance scaled by its days:

Then come the per-project lines, the plan-change lines as stored, and the next month's fee in advance. For a dollar book, convert turns each line's cents into kobo and computes VAT and the expected WHT on the kobo subtotal.

Plan changes. ChangePlan treats a higher monthly fee, or any move to annual, as an upgrade applied at once; anything else waits for the period's end unless immediately. Prorate counts days, the day of the change on the new plan: Pro → Team on 13 October (19 of 31 days) is −$6.13 and +$24.52. The lines are stored with the change and land on that month's invoice; setPlan also sets the quota plan unless the admin chose another.

Invoices and credit notes

One invoice per organisation and month (UNIQUE (org_id, period_start)). Invoicing drafts the current month on its last day and handles the previous one once the watermark has passed midnight on the 1st:

Draft re-rates a draft and replaces its lines. IssueDue does nothing until the admin turns automatic issue on. Issue, in one transaction, converts a dollar draft at a fresh rate (convertDraft, the whole quote kept in invoices.fx), takes the year's next number XB-YYYY-NNNNNN from a counter row (NextBillingNumber, so a rollback gives the number back), freezes both parties' details, posts invoiceTxn (Dr receivable, Cr revenue per line and VAT) and applies any credit held. After commit it emails the contacts and debits the wallet if consented. PDFs are rendered on request from the frozen invoice (InvoicePDF). VAT (7.5%) and WHT (5%) are settings.

CreditNote is admin-only: an amount before VAT, VAT at the invoice's rate, at most the invoice's total across its notes. creditNoteTxn debits revenue in proportion to the invoice's lines and VAT, and credits what is still owed, the rest to credit_balance.

Immutability is in the queries

Unlike price books and the ledger, issued invoices have no trigger. Every query that writes an invoice or its lines (UpdateDraftInvoice, SetDraftLineNaira, DeleteInvoiceLines, IssueInvoice) requires status = 'draft'; after issue only settlement columns change.

Risk

A month that fails to issue is never retried. Invoicing writes billing.invoiced_through whatever DraftAll and IssueDue returned, and monthDone then skips the month. A draft refused for a stale rate, a provider outage or a database error stays a draft, and an organisation whose rating failed gets none. A scratch test with a stale rate on the first run and a fresh one two hours later left the draft in place, although docs/billing.md says "the next hourly run tries again". The status page shows "Last month's invoices are late" (Health), but until the admin issues by hand nothing is collected.

Spec and code differ

V3 §3.6 issues drafts automatically on the 1st. Automatic issue is off until the admin turns it on (decision M20 #10).

The rate. Quote asks the wallet provider for USD→NGN. A strict quote, for issuing, refuses one whose fetched_at is older than fx_max_age_hours (4 hours), because the provider keeps answering with its last rate when its feed fails. Drafts and estimates reuse a quote for two minutes and accept any age. The provider sets the spread; the quote on the invoice is the only record of it.

Wallet debits

Each organisation's money sits in its own wallet at the wallet provider, funded by transfer to a permanent virtual account (EnsureWallet: tier 2, a director's BVN forwarded and not kept). A verified deposit is posted Dr cash:iswallet, Cr wallet_balance, and settles nothing (recordPayment). With an owner's consent (AcceptDebitTerms), the wallet is debited on issue, on each deposit, on dunning retries and for what is already owed; without it, on Pay from wallet (PayInvoiceFromWallet). chargeInvoiceFromWallet:

  1. Resolves any earlier attempt that got no answer by its key; while one is unresolved, stops.
  2. Reads what the invoice owes and the wallet's available balance. Short is a decline: all or nothing, and dunning starts.
  3. Inserts a payment_intents row with a fresh key; a partial unique index allows one pending wallet debit per invoice (payment_intents_one_wallet_debit).
  4. Transfers to Xenibase's operating wallet with the invoice number as narration; a 200 is final.
  5. settleWalletDebit: Dr wallet_balance, Cr receivable, and Cr credit_balance for anything no longer owed.
Risk

A terminal request is booked as a debit whatever its status. This branch and resolveWalletIntent settle once RequestState.Terminal is true and never read Status; docs/iswallet.md only promises that a 404 means nothing posted. If the provider records a transfer that failed after a timeout as terminal and failed, the invoice is marked paid with no money moved. A scratch test with such a provider issued a ₦32,572.50 invoice as paid while the wallet's balance stayed the same. The ledger audit passes; only the nightly balance reconciliation shows it.

Risk

Two attempts that are never both pending both debit. An attempt reads what is owed before inserting its intent. If another attempt inserts, transfers and settles in between, the first inserts its own intent (nothing is pending now) and transfers again; the second amount is booked as credit. The triggers can coincide: a deposit's afterDeposit goroutine, the button, the daily dunning on every server. A scratch test with one slow balance read debited ₦65,145 for a ₦32,572.50 invoice. The credit pays the next invoice, but Refund returns only manual payments, so it can never go back to the wallet.

Risk

Debits continue under superseded terms. DebitTermsVersion is to change with the wording so organisations "are asked again", and ConsentOf reports Current: false, but automatic debits only check that debit_consent_at is set.

Webhooks, re-query and reconciliation

ParseWebhook requires X-iSpend-Signature to equal Sign, an HMAC-SHA256 over "<timestamp>.<body>" compared in constant time, with the timestamp within 5 minutes; otherwise 401. Only wallet.credit.posted from a va_deposit is acted on, keyed by data.txn_id, at amount_credited (net of the provider's fee).

The body is only a hint (decision M21 #2). HandleEvent stores the event once per (provider, event id), and settleVerified reads the transaction back and attributes it by the virtual account the provider names; with none it is unmatched until the admin attributes it. Requery lists the provider's transactions for the last two hours hourly and the previous day nightly, posts what is missing and resolves pending debits; Reconcile then compares transactions with payments and each wallet's balance with wallet_balance. Reversals are logged and ignored, as docs/iswallet.md lists under "Still open".

Risk

An event that fails to process gets one more try. A failure answers 500 "the event will be retried", but the row is stored, so the provider's retry is a duplicate answered 200. The re-query uses its own id, requery:<ref>, stored the same way: if it fails too, later re-queries skip it. A scratch test with the verify call failing twice left a ₦9,860 deposit unbooked after two re-queries. Debits still work (they read the provider's balance), but wallet_balance stays short and no admin action re-processes a failed event.

Manual payments and refunds. RecordManual records a bank payment under a reference that cannot repeat, settling the named invoice, then the oldest, then credit; one short by exactly the expected WHT settles as paid_wht_pending until the certificate is uploaded. Refund returns at most what is left of a manual payment, from credit_balance, first taking it back off the invoices it settled when asked (unapply).

Spec and code differ

V3 §3.7 matches a transfer for the total minus WHT. With wallets a deposit settles nothing and a debit takes the full amount owed, so an organisation that deducts WHT pays it too; the match applies only to manual payments.

Dunning, budgets and the spend cap

Daily runs after 01:00 UTC and ends with RunDunning. A declined automatic debit is retried on days 3, 5 and 7; a provider outage puts the retry back instead of counting it. The ladder counts from the oldest unpaid due date: day 0 overdue, 3 restricted, 7 final notice, 10 suspended, 40 deletion scheduled with 7 days' notice. Each step is claimed in dunning_steps under UNIQUE (org_id, cycle, step) before it acts. Free organisations are never suspended; the admin can hold the ladder; paying everything reverses it (Reevaluate). RefreshForecast projects the month hourly, emails at 50, 80 and 100% of the budget, and sets capped when the projected usage charges (overage, dedicated, add-ons) reach the cap.

StateEffect
Restricted (day 3) or suspendedNew projects, branches, dedicated creates and replicas refused (CheckBillingStanding)
Suspended (day 10)V2 suspension, reason billing: …; dedicated projects deleted at day 47 only if delete_for_non_payment is on
CappedNew branches, dedicated instances, replicas, HA and add-ons refused (CheckSpendCap); webhook deliveries wait; job runs skipped; the edge cuts data and storage rate limits to a quarter and refuses new transforms and realtime connections, sparing auth (gateway)

Nothing running stops, so a dedicated instance keeps billing past the cap: the cap pauses growth, it does not limit the bill.

Spec and code differ

V3 §3.8 schedules paid resources for deletion on day 40; the code deletes only if the admin opted in (decision M21 #10).

The Free tier

Activity is clients seen through the poolers each minute, and console use (TouchProjectsActive, which also clears the pause warning); Xenibase's own sessions bypass the poolers. Sweep wakes projects of organisations that now pay; then, only if the waker accepts a connection (WakerReachable), warns active Free shared projects idle 6 days and queues pause_project at 7 days and 24 hours after the warning (FreeProjectsIdleSince), and archive_project after 90 days paused. Archived projects get notices 30 and 7 days before a year, then are deleted (sweepArchived). Notices are claimed by conditional updates, so several servers send each once.

runPause re-checks activity, points the route at the waker first, then sets ALLOW_CONNECTIONS false and ends the sessions. The waker () calls Wake and refuses with 08004 and "retry in about 30 seconds". runArchive takes an archive backup, which backup.Archive verifies by restoring it into a scratch database, marks the project archived and drops the database; roles stay, so runUnarchive restores it under the same connection string. A retry reuses an earlier attempt's backup:

Risk

A retry drops the database on an unverified backup. backup.Archive marks the row succeeded before verifyRestore runs, and deletes it only if verification returns an error and the delete works. If the server stops during verification (a deploy, a crash), or the delete fails, the next attempt finds the row through BackupForOperation and goes straight to DROP DATABASE … WITH (FORCE), against V3 §4.3 and decision M22 #5. Read from the code, not reproduced.

Spec and code differ

Decision M22 #2 pauses only Free projects of active organisations, re-checked when the operation runs. runPause and runArchive re-check activity, not the plan, so a project whose organisation upgraded after the sweep is paused or archived and then woken by the next sweep.

Abuse limits on open signup (Turnstile, three accounts a day per IP, verified email, TOTP, one personal organisation) are on .

Cost attribution and margins

Attribute divides each UTC day's cost among organisations, in each cost's currency: a node's monthly price over the month's days, by dedicated instances' vCPU share (standbys as ha), the rest to its shared projects half by storage and half by connection-hours, unused as idle; edge nodes by API requests and realtime minutes; storage, transfer and messages by use; floating IPs and overheads to nobody. Rates for costs are naira per unit, entered by hand with history (RatesAt), separate from the provider's invoice rate. Margins sets a month's cost at today's rate and at each day's (the difference is FX erosion) against what it earns (earned).

Reporting only

Attribution's float64 moves no money. Margin revenue re-rates the month at the organisation's current book and today's rate rather than reading the issued invoice, so after a repricing a past month differs from what was invoiced.

Capacity automation

Evaluate runs hourly per region and tier, skipping a tier with a node already provisioning: shared disk projected past 70% within 14 days (a week's slope per node); no healthy dedicated host with room for the largest profile; edges above 70% CPU in 11 of the hour's 12 buckets. propose prices a server from the provider's catalog and inserts a proposal; a partial unique index allows one open proposal per region and tier. It starts by itself only with a provider that creates servers, automatic provisioning on, and room in the budget:

The budget is zero by default, so nothing is bought until one is set; otherwise the proposal waits for Approve, behind step-up auth (decision M24 #6). A dedicated create that finds no host calls HostFor, which joins the region's proposal and waits for its node, or answers 409 capacity_pending_approval.

Risk

The budget is checked one proposal at a time. NodeCostTotals sums existing nodes, and a started proposal has no node until its operation runs proposalNode. One Evaluate over several regions and tiers, or simultaneous HostFor calls, see the same headroom and all start. A scratch test with nodes costing 1,500, a budget of 3,000 and four 1,000 proposals started all four: 5,500 a month. The index bounds this to one server per region and tier at a time, so it does not run away, but the budget is not the ceiling it is described as.

runProvision creates the node with a one-time token, the server with the token in its cloud-init (found again by label on a retry), waits 20 minutes for the agent, creates a shared node's cluster and puts the node into service. On failure failProvision deletes the server and node if nothing runs there. Drain queues a move per project; advanceMoves runs one at a time through the zero-downtime move (), skipping HA, paused and archived projects. Rebalance proposes up to ten moves a week from the fullest to the emptiest shared node while their disk use differs by more than 15%. sweepEmpty deletes a provider's server that has held nothing for 24 hours (NodeOccupancy: projects, archived ones included, dedicated instances, kept move copies, HA and etcd members), unless marked keep.

Risk

The empty-node sweep destroys the server before checking again. Occupancy is read once per sweep; deleteNode then deletes the server, retires its shared clusters, and only then calls RemoveNode, whose instance check passes because the clusters were just retired. Placement does not exclude empty nodes and prefers the emptiest cluster, so a project placed there in between loses its database. The window is short.

Spec and code differ

V3 §5.3 evens out disk and CPU; the rebalancer compares disk only.

Legal documents

Each person accepts the terms, privacy notice and AUP through the terms gate (). Each organisation accepts the SLA, the DPA and its own order form: Publish numbers versions per kind and organisation, the latest is in effect, and Accept takes only that version, recording the owner and IP once. A plan change may carry accept_legal (ChangeOrgPlan), and Billing shows a banner while anything is outstanding.

Spec and code differ

Paid plans work without accepting the SLA and DPA (decision M23 #9): V3 §7.3's acceptance is a record, not a gate.
